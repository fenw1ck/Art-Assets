Why the game engine does what it does, and doesn't do the rest. Compared part by part with Godot, Unity, Unreal Engine, CryEngine and GameMaker (25 Nov 2026).

Art libraryGame engineWhy the engine is built this way

The short version

Our engine (engine.js) is a rules engine for small browser games, not a general-purpose engine. The Euclid game is one HTML page that runs anywhere, offline, with nothing to install and no image or sound files. Everything is drawn and composed in code. That one fact decides most of what follows.

  • We copy the big engines' ideas, not their size. From Unreal and Unity: input as actions in contexts. From Godot: the scene tree and signals. From every engine: the fixed-step game loop. From Sequencer and AnimationPlayer: timelines.
  • We skip what a single-player, hand-drawn, one-file game doesn't need. That means networking, threads, a physics solver, a 3-D renderer, and an editor.
  • Look and behaviour are kept apart. engine.js holds rules and numbers only. core.js and the art files hold the drawing. The skeleton is geometry the art paints.

What the five engines agree on, and what we took

  1. Fixed-step rules, free-rate drawing. Godot runs physics 60 times a second, Unity's FixedUpdate every 0.02 s, and GameMaker steps at the room speed. → ENGINE.loop.
  2. Input is about intent, not keys. Unreal's Input Actions and Mapping Contexts, Unity's action maps and Godot's InputMap all separate "jump" from "Space". → ENGINE.input.
  3. Things are trees or bags of parts. Godot nodes, Unity GameObjects with components, Unreal Actors with components, CryEngine entities, GameMaker objects in rooms. → ENGINE.scene (a tree, the simplest of these).
  4. Systems talk by events. → ENGINE.signals.
  5. Motion is data on a timeline. Sequencer, Timeline, AnimationPlayer. → ENGINE.timeline, ENGINE.skeleton.
  6. Text is looked up, not written into code. → ENGINE.l10n.

What the game does with it now (25 Nov 2026)

  • Looking around: press L (or hold the right mouse button) and move the mouse. Moving it forward looks up. In look mode a crosshair appears and a click acts on what's under it. Esc stops.
  • The map is a house plan: walled rooms with their floors (stone corridors, wooden libraries and bedrooms, tiled kitchens, glass greenhouses), doors and passages. The corner of the walking screen shows the same plan around you, with a circle where you are.
  • The Papyrus Map has a Use button that marks creatures, unopened chests and the way to the nearest shop.
  • The shop shows each item's name, price and use when you hover over it or tap it.
