# Birds

## Parts (bird topography)

- **Bill:** upper mandible (maxilla) and lower mandible. The culmen is the ridge of the upper mandible and the gonys is the ridge under the lower mandible. The tomia are the cutting edges, and the nostrils (nares) sit near the base of the upper mandible.
- **Head:** forehead, crown, nape, lores (between eye and bill), eye-ring, ear-coverts (auriculars), malar (cheek stripe area), chin and throat. Markings: supercilium (eyebrow), eyestripe, moustachial stripe.
- **Body:** mantle and back, scapulars (over the shoulder), rump, breast, belly, flanks, vent (crissum; the cloaca opens here), undertail coverts.
- **Wing:**
  - flight feathers (remiges): primaries on the "hand", usually 9–10, numbered P1–P10 outward; secondaries on the forearm; tertials nearest the body;
  - coverts over their bases: greater, median, lesser, primary coverts;
  - the alula, a small feather group at the bend of the wing.
- **Tail:** tail feathers (rectrices) attach at the base and fan out from it, with the uppertail and undertail coverts over the base.
- **Legs and feet:** tarsus, toes (the hind toe is the hallux), claws.
- **Feather types:** contour feathers form the smooth outer covering (flight feathers are contour feathers); down feathers are soft and fluffy, with no locked vane.
- **Feather parts:** the calamus (hollow quill base), the rachis (shaft) and the vanes, made of barbs. Pennaceous barbs lock together into a vane; plumulaceous (downy) barbs don't.
- Feathers grow in tracts and overlap like roof tiles. At rest, the closed wing often hides the lesser coverts and alula under the body feathers and scapulars.

## Distinguishing features (what separates species)

Bill shape, length and colour; head shape; body proportions (smaller birds tend to have relatively bigger heads); wing shape and length, and how far the primaries project past the tertials; tail shape and length; leg length; colours and markings, described feather group by feather group; typical posture.

## Construction order (John Muir Laws)

1. A line for the posture (the angle of head and body).
2. An oval for the body, then a smaller one for the head, sized against the body.
3. Carve in the angles where the head and tail join the body.
4. The eye-to-bill line, then the bill.
5. Lightly outline the feather groups (wing coverts, tertials, primaries, tail) before any markings, then place the markings on those groups.

## States (likely)

perched, flying (wings spread, so the primaries, secondaries and coverts all show), possibly juvenile vs adult plumage, and seasonal plumage.
