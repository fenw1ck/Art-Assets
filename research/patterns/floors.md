# Floor layouts

## Distinguishing features

- **Opus tessellatum:** a mosaic of small units, all the same size and shape.
- **Opus sectile:** larger pieces of marble (also glass, mother-of-pearl) cut to shape and fitted together. Geometric panels of squares, triangles and octagons, often in strongly contrasting stones such as giallo antico, portasanta and breccia, in square-in-square designs within a grid.
- **Cosmatesque (opus alexandrinum):** medieval Italian geometric inlay of marble with glass mosaic. Bands, roundels and panels of fine pattern, alternating with plain white marble bands. The Great Pavement at Westminster Abbey is one.
- **Checkerboard and ashlar:** square or rectangular slabs on a grid.

## Rendering notes

- Draw the layout grid first, then each stone with its own material and seed, then the seams, then wear.
- Seams: grout sits darker and slightly lower than the stone. One edge catches the light and the other shades (occlusion).
- Avoid visible repeats with seeded variation per stone or with Wang tiles.
