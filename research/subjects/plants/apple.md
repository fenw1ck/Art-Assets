# Apple (semi-dwarf) (Malus domestica)

## Distinguishing features

- **Growth architecture:** Small to medium deciduous tree trained to a central leader; semi-dwarf size is determined mainly by rootstock and pruning.
- **Leaves:** Alternate simple elliptic-ovate leaves with irregular saw-toothed margins; often hairy beneath; petioles moderate length.
- **Stem and branching:** Woody trunk with scaffold branches and short fruiting spurs. Flower buds develop at tips of spurs; spurs are short, knobbly shoots distinct from long vegetative shoots.
- **Flowers:** Clusters of about 4–6 five-petalled flowers at spur tips, white to pink; central king bloom often opens first.
- **Fruit:** Pome develops from flowers on spurs; persistent calyx may remain at distal end. Fruit colour varies by cultivar.

## Renderer construction rules

Build a woody central leader and scaffold branches before foliage. Distribute alternate serrate leaves along shoots; place short spur shoots on older wood and blossom clusters at their tips. Fruit should occupy the same spur positions as flowers, not hang from every leaf node.
