# Petunia (*Petunia axillaris* as architectural reference)

## Distinguishing features
- Gently mounding to trailing, spreading and branched habit.
- Simple leaves, generally ovate, arranged alternately; leaves and stems can be softly hairy/sticky depending on species and cultivar.
- Large solitary trumpet/funnel-shaped flowers arise in upper leaf axils, not in composite heads or terminal racemes.
- The corolla has a flared limb and fused tube; five shallow lobes form the rim.

## Construction rules
1. Build a low, spreading branched framework that can spill over a container rim; do not use tendrils.
2. Place simple alternate leaves along the shoots.
3. Put solitary trumpet flowers in upper leaf axils; angle each flower outward/upward with a visible fused tube and five-lobed rim.
4. Immature: foliage and buds only. Mature: axillary flowers across the upper growth. Past flowering: reduce open trumpets, retain leaves and show a few spent/collapsed corollas.
5. Seed branch length, internodes, leaf size, flower angle and colour; preserve the axillary solitary flower placement.
