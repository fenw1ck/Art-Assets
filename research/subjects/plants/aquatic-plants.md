# Aquatic plants

Aquatic habit is an ecological growth form, not a single taxonomic lineage. Aquatic species in this entry must retain their actual family and order in the taxonomy fields of their infocards.

## Growth-form distinctions

- **Submerged:** most vegetative surfaces remain underwater; leaves may be narrow, ribbon-like, finely divided, or reduced. Reproductive structures may reach the surface or remain submerged.
- **Floating-leaved, rooted:** rhizomes or rooted stems anchor the plant; long flexible petioles support blades at the water surface. Do not draw these as free-floating plants.
- **Free-floating:** the plant is not rooted in substrate; buoyancy comes from small floating bodies, air spaces, or inflated tissues. Roots may hang freely in the water.
- **Emergent:** roots and lower stems are submerged or in saturated substrate while leaves and reproductive axes rise above water.
- **Amphibious/marginal:** vegetative architecture changes with water depth and exposure; record submerged and aerial leaf forms separately when dimorphic.

## Construction requirements

Record root attachment, rhizome or stolon form, leaf position relative to the waterline, petiole length and flexibility, leaf aeration, blade shape and venation, inflorescence position, and fruit/seed dispersal. Water level is a structural reference plane in the plate, not a decorative background.

## Distinguishing examples

- *Nymphaea odorata*: rooted rhizomatous water lily; long petioles carry floating, rounded blades with a basal sinus; flowers float or rise just above the surface.
- *Nuphar variegata*: rooted rhizomatous pond-lily; floating leaves are typically more elongate; flowers are raised above the water on stout peduncles.
- *Nelumbo lutea*: rooted; peltate leaves with centrally attached petioles held above water; large emergent flowers and distinctive flat-topped receptacle.
- *Lemna minor*: free-floating minute fronds, commonly with a single root per frond; no conventional stem or foliage canopy.
- *Pistia stratiotes*: free-floating rosette with thick, ribbed, velvety leaves and hanging fibrous roots.
- *Elodea canadensis*: submerged branching shoots with small leaves in whorls; flowers may reach the surface on slender stalks.
- *Vallisneria americana*: submerged ribbon leaves in basal rosettes; female flowers reach the surface on long coiled stalks.
- *Sagittaria latifolia*: emergent/marginal rosette; aerial leaves commonly arrow-shaped, with separate male and female flowers in whorls on an erect scape.
- *Typha latifolia*: emergent rhizomatous perennial; long linear leaves sheath the base; dense cylindrical male and female spike portions form a distinctive brown cattail head.
