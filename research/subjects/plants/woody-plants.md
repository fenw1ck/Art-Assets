# Woody plants — conifers and broadleaf trees

## Eastern white pine — *Pinus strobus*

- **Architecture:** evergreen conifer; young trees are pyramidal with branches in annual whorls, while older crowns broaden into horizontal and ascending branches.
- **Leaves:** soft, slender needles in fascicles of five; pale stomatal lines occur on inner faces.
- **Reproduction:** pollen cones and ovulate cones occur on the same tree. Seed cones are pendent, cylindrical and mature in the second year.
- **References:** Oregon State University, [Landscape Plants: *Pinus strobus*](https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/pinus-strobus), supports five-needle fascicles, branch architecture and cones; Minnesota DNR, [Eastern white pine](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/trees/eastern-white-pine.html), corroborates whorled branching, needle characters and cone maturation.

## Red maple — *Acer rubrum*

- **Architecture:** deciduous tree; forest-grown trees commonly develop a clear trunk, while open-grown trees develop a rounded crown.
- **Leaves:** opposite, simple, palmately veined, usually three to five lobed, with serrate margins.
- **Reproduction:** small flowers occur in drooping axillary clusters, often before leaves; fruit is a paired winged samara.
- **References:** USDA Forest Service FEIS, [*Acer rubrum*](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/acerub), supports tree habit, flowers and samaras; University of Rhode Island-hosted Virginia Tech fact sheet, [*Acer rubrum*](https://web.uri.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2078/Acer-rubrum.pdf), supports opposite lobed leaves and flower/fruit details.

## White oak — *Quercus alba*

- **Architecture:** large, spreading, long-lived deciduous tree.
- **Leaves:** alternate, simple, obovate to oblong, with rounded lobes and pinnate venation; lobes lack bristle tips.
- **Reproduction:** monoecious; yellowish staminate catkins and reddish pistillate spikes occur on new growth. Acorns are generally paired; the cup encloses roughly one-third to one-half of the nut.
- **References:** USDA Forest Service FEIS, [*Quercus alba*](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/quealb), supports tree habit, flower and acorn traits; UF/IFAS, [White oak](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/ST541), supports leaf morphology and cup proportions.

## Water oak — *Quercus nigra*

- **Architecture:** fast-growing tree with a rounded, spreading crown; deciduous in the northern part of its range and semi-evergreen in the Deep South.
- **Leaves:** alternate, simple, pinnately veined and highly variable. The common outline is spatulate: narrow at the base and broader toward the apex. Leaves may be entire or shallowly three-lobed, sometimes with bristle tips. Do not render every water oak leaf as a classic deeply lobed oak leaf.
- **Reproduction:** monoecious; male catkins and female spikes emerge shortly before or with new leaves. Acorns mature at the end of the second growing season.
- **Variation:** juvenile leaves can be more deeply lobed than mature leaves; leaf shape varies substantially within a single tree.
- **References:** UF/IFAS, [*Quercus nigra*: Water Oak](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/ST553), supports arrangement, leaf type, variable shape, venation, flowers and crown; USDA Forest Service FEIS, [Water oak](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/quenig), supports habit, seasonal development and second-year acorn maturation; University of Alabama Arboretum, [Water Oak](https://arboretum.ua.edu/whats-growing-on/water-oak-quercus-nigra/), supports spatulate leaf diagnosis and ecological fruiting; Oregon State University, [Landscape Plants: *Quercus nigra*](https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/quercus-nigra), documents within-species and juvenile leaf variation.

## Black willow — *Salix nigra*

- **Architecture:** fast-growing deciduous tree, sometimes shrubby; broad open crown, often leaning or divided trunks, deeply furrowed bark and shallow laterally extensive roots.
- **Leaves and flowers:** species account supports narrow leaves and catkin-bearing willow habit; precise leaf-margin and flower-placement traits should remain provisional until an exact regional flora is added.
- **References:** USDA Forest Service FEIS, [*Salix nigra*](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/salnig), supports growth form, crown, trunk, bark, twigs and root habit.

## Construction rules

- Conifers have needle or scale leaves and seed cones; do not give them broad angiosperm leaves or flowers.
- Tree architecture must reflect species habit and age. Whorled pine branches, opposite maple branching and oak branching are not interchangeable.
- Oak leaf shape is species-specific. In particular, *Quercus nigra* has variable spatulate leaves and should not be generalized from white oak.
- Keep juvenile and adult leaf forms distinct when sources document ontogenetic variation.

## Douglas-fir — *Pseudotsuga menziesii*

- **Architecture:** large evergreen conifer. Open-grown young trees retain low branches; old forest trees develop tall clear boles and narrow crowns.
- **Leaves:** yellow-green needles arranged singly in a spiral around branchlets.
- **Cones:** pendent oblong seed cones, commonly 2–4 inches, with distinctive three-pointed bracts. This diagnostic feature distinguishes Douglas-fir from true firs.
- **References:** USDA Forest Service FEIS, [Coast Douglas-fir](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/psemenm), supports crown, bark, foliage and cone position; UC ANR, [Douglas-fir](https://ucanr.edu/site/forest-research-and-outreach/douglas-fir-pseudotsuga-menziesii), documents spiral needles and three-pointed bracts.

## Bald cypress — *Taxodium distichum*

- **Architecture:** deciduous conifer; young crown pyramidal, mature crown broad and spreading. Wet-site trees may develop woody knees from roots.
- **Leaves:** small, flat, linear leaves on feathery deciduous branchlets; foliage turns coppery before dropping.
- **Cones:** rounded woody seed cones; male and female reproductive structures are separate but occur on the same tree.
- **References:** UF/IFAS, [Baldcypress](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/ST620), supports leaf arrangement, type, margin, shape, cones, crown and knees; UF Gardening Solutions, [Bald Cypress](https://gardeningsolutions.ifas.ufl.edu/mastergardener/resources/plantid/ornamentals/bald-cypress/), corroborates branchlet, bark and buttress features.

## Bur oak — *Quercus macrocarpa*

- **Architecture:** large deciduous tree with broad rounded crown; may be shrubby near range margins. Branchlets commonly have corky wings.
- **Leaves:** alternate, simple, fiddle-shaped and obovate; lower half has several deep lobes while the upper half has fewer shallow lobes.
- **Reproduction:** monoecious catkins; large acorns with a thick, fringed cup covering roughly one-half to three-quarters of the nut. Acorns mature in the first year.
- **Variation:** USDA NRCS recognizes multiple varieties with differences in stature and cup size; preserve this variation rather than treating every tree as identical.
- **References:** USDA NRCS, [Bur Oak Plant Guide](https://plants.sc.egov.usda.gov/DocumentLibrary/plantguide/pdf/pg_quma2.pdf), supports morphology, reproductive structures and intraspecific variation.

## Southern live oak — *Quercus virginiana*

- **Architecture:** massive evergreen to semi-evergreen oak, often wider than tall, with low, broad, winding and drooping limbs.
- **Leaves:** alternate/spiral, simple, stiff and leathery, elliptic to ovate; margins revolute and usually entire, though new growth may be toothed or shallowly lobed.
- **Reproduction:** male yellow-green catkins and small female spikes; acorns with a shallow cup.
- **References:** UF/IFAS, [Shade-tree identification key](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/EP510), documents leaf variation, bark, flowers and acorns; UF Gardening Solutions, [Live Oak](https://gardeningsolutions.ifas.ufl.edu/mastergardener/resources/plantid/ornamentals/live-oak/), documents evergreen habit, branch architecture and reproductive structures.

## Paper birch — *Betula papyrifera*

- **Architecture:** deciduous tree with an open crown; mature bark is thin, white and papery, peeling in sheets. Sapling bark is reddish brown.
- **Reproduction:** monoecious, with separate pendulous male and female catkins. Male catkins overwinter; female catkins emerge in spring. Fruits are tiny winged nutlets.
- **Evidence limit:** the cited USDA account strongly supports bark, roots, catkins and fruit; leaf outline and serration need an additional taxon-specific flora source before the card is considered complete.
- **References:** USDA Forest Service FEIS, [Paper birch](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/betpap).
