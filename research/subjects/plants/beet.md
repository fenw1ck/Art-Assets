# Garden beet (Beta vulgaris Conditiva Group)

## Distinguishing features

- **Growth architecture:** Biennial root crop; basal rosette above a swollen hypocotyl and upper taproot.
- **Leaves:** Basal leaves alternate in a rosette, long-petiolate, ovate to cordate, often wavy and puckered; red or purple veins may be prominent.
- **Stem and branching:** Short vegetative crown above a swollen storage organ. In the second year, a tall branched flowering stem rises from the crown.
- **Flowers:** Small greenish flowers in clusters along a long branched paniculate spike; not showy.
- **Fruit or harvest organ:** Seed units are grouped in persistent perianth bases into rough seedballs (glomerules); edible beet is a swollen hypocotyl/root, not a fruit.

## Renderer construction rules

Show the swollen root at the soil line, with a narrowing taproot below and a basal rosette above. Petioles emerge from the crown. Do not give it chard's oversized colourful petioles as the primary feature. Add tall green flowering stems only in seed stage.
