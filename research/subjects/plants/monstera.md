# Monstera (Monstera deliciosa)

## Distinguishing features

- **Growth architecture:** Climbing hemiepiphyte with thick, sparsely branching stem; climbs using long aerial roots.
- **Leaves:** Juvenile leaves are small, entire and heart-shaped; mature leaves become very large, leathery, deeply split and perforated (fenestrated), with long thick petioles.
- **Stem and branching:** Thick cylindrical stem with prominent leaf scars; long aerial roots emerge at nodes, grow downward and attach to supports or soil. Leaves alternate at nodes.
- **Flowers:** Creamy spadix surrounded by a boat-shaped white to cream spathe; typical of Araceae and uncommon on indoor houseplants.
- **Fruit or propagules:** Elongated compound infructescence of tightly packed berry-like segments; ripens slowly and is rarely produced indoors.

## Renderer construction rules

Model leaf ontogeny explicitly: juvenile entire heart leaves first, then progressively larger leaves with splits and internal holes. A mature plant needs a thick climbing stem, long petioles and aerial roots at nodes. Do not make every leaf equally fenestrated on a young plant.
