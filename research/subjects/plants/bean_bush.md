# Bush bean (Phaseolus vulgaris (bush growth-habit cultivars))

## Distinguishing features

- **Growth architecture:** Compact erect or semi-erect herbaceous annual; determinate or semi-determinate branching varies by cultivar.
- **Leaves:** Alternate, long-petioled trifoliate leaves. Three broad ovate leaflets; terminal leaflet largest; entire margins, pinnate venation.
- **Stem and branching:** Several slender, often slightly hairy stems branch from the lower nodes; no twining axis and no tendrils in the typical bush form.
- **Flowers:** Small axillary or terminal racemes; papilionaceous pea-type flowers, often paired or solitary along the rachis; white, pink, lilac or purple.
- **Fruit:** Each pollinated flower produces one elongated pod; pods may be round or flattened, straight or curved, and contain several seeds.

## Renderer construction rules

Build a low, self-supporting branching bush. Attach each trifoliate leaf to a node on a long petiole, with three distinct leaflets. Place short racemes in leaf axils and pods where flowers occurred. Do not draw climbing coils or pea tendrils.
