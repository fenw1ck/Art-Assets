# Bryophytes (mosses and liverworts)

Mosses and liverworts are non-vascular land plants with a dominant gametophyte generation. They are not miniature flowering plants and must not inherit the ordinary vascular-plant construction assumptions.

## Moss architecture

- The visible plant is a leafy gametophyte, commonly with leaves arranged around the stem in more than two rows.
- Rhizoids anchor the gametophyte; they are not true roots.
- The sporophyte is attached to and nutritionally dependent on the female gametophyte. It commonly comprises foot, seta, and capsule; capsule architecture, operculum, and peristome vary among lineages.
- *Sphagnum* has specialized hyaline water-storage cells and living photosynthetic cells; branches are arranged in fascicles around an upright stem, with a capitulum at the apex.
- *Polytrichum* has erect shoots, a conspicuous central costa, and photosynthetic lamellae on the upper leaf surface; sporophytes have a hairy calyptra.
- *Hypnum* commonly forms creeping, branching mats with overlapping leaves and curved capsules.

## Liverwort architecture

- Thalloid liverworts have a flattened, often dichotomously branching thallus; leafy liverworts have flattened stems with two lateral leaf rows and commonly a third ventral row of underleaves.
- Liverwort leaves lack a costa; many are bilobed or divided. Rhizoids are single-celled.
- *Marchantia polymorpha* is a thalloid liverwort with dorsal air pores, gemma cups, and stalked antheridiophores/archegoniophores.
- *Riccia fluitans* is an aquatic or amphibious thalloid liverwort; floating forms can be finely divided and lack the robust terrestrial rosette appearance.
- *Frullania* is a leafy liverwort with bilobed leaves; the smaller ventral lobe is modified into a water-sac (lobule).

## Rendering requirements

Show the gametophyte as the main plant body. If a sporophyte is present, attach it to the gametophyte and show its true proportions. Use a magnified inset for diagnostic leaf cells, hyaline cells, lamellae, underleaves, or capsule teeth where needed; do not exaggerate microscopic details in the whole-plant silhouette.
