# Garden pansy (*Viola × wittrockiana*)

## Distinguishing features
- Compact, low, rounded/mounded herb; some cultivars spread or trail.
- Simple alternate leaves, usually ovate to elliptic/lanceolate, with crenate or dentate margins.
- Large, flattened, bilaterally symmetrical flowers, usually solitary on individual stalks from leaf axils.
- Five overlapping petals: two upper, two lateral and one larger lower petal. The lower petal often has a spur; petals frequently carry a central blotch/eyespot or contrasting face pattern.
- Flowers are not radial five-petal stars; retain the upper/lateral/lower arrangement and bilateral symmetry.

## Construction rules
1. Build a compact crown with several short leafy shoots, not a long climbing or trailing vine.
2. Place alternate leaves along short stems; vary leaf size and stalk length, retaining toothed margins.
3. Raise single flowers above the foliage on separate pedicels. Keep the face toward the viewer and preserve bilateral petal structure.
4. Immature: compact foliage, unopened buds. Mature: staggered open flowers above leaves. Past flowering: fewer open blooms, bent pedicels and spent flowers while the mound remains.
5. Seed variation across mound width, leaf count, flower colour/pattern and stalk angles; do not alter the five-petal architecture.
