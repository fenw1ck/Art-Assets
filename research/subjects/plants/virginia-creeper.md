# Virginia creeper (*Parthenocissus quinquefolia*)

## Distinguishing features

- **Leaves:** palmately compound, usually **five leaflets** (three on young growth, sometimes seven) from one point on a long stalk; leaflets elliptic, pointed, coarsely toothed mostly above the middle; 10–20 cm across. Deep red to crimson in autumn. (Five leaflets, not poison ivy's three.)
- **Tendrils:** branched 3–8 times, each tip a small sticky disc (about 5 mm) that grips smooth walls.
- **Stems:** young stems red to green with reddish lenticels; older bark grey-brown, hairy with aerial roots (bright orange-brown while growing fast).
- **Fruit:** blue-black berries about 6 mm across, in clusters on red stalks, late summer to autumn.

## Construction order

Stems from the frame (hairy aerial roots on old stems); five-leaflet leaves on red stalks; disc tendrils at about half the nodes; berry clusters on red branched stalks, or the whole vine in autumn red.

## States

- **full**, **damaged**, **cut**; colour variant **autumn**.
