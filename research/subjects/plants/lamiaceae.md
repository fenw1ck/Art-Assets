# Mint family

## Parts

- **Square stems.**
- **Leaves** in opposite pairs, each pair turned a quarter turn from the last (decussate). In side view, every other pair faces the viewer.
- **Side shoots** grow from the leaf angles.
- **Flowers** grow in whorls up a spike at each tip.

## Distinguishing features

- **Basil:** one upright stem; large, glossy, slightly cupped leaves; white flower spikes.
- **Mint:** many spreading stems; toothed, wrinkled leaves; lilac spikes.
- **Thyme:** many thin woody stems; tiny narrow leaves; small pink clusters.
- **Lavender:** a woody, grey-green shrublet; narrow, rolled-edged, downy leaves; long bare flower stalks ending in dense purple spikes held well above the leaves.
- **Oregano:** a low, spreading mat; small rounded-oval leaves, softly hairy; flat-topped clusters of tiny pink-purple flowers among papery bracts.
- **Rosemary:** an upright evergreen shrub; needle-like leaves, dark above and white below, crowded along woody stems; small pale blue two-lipped flowers along the stems.
- **Sage:** a woody-based shrub; oblong grey-green leaves with a pebbled (rugose) surface; violet-blue two-lipped flowers in whorled spikes.

## Mint (Mentha spicata), in detail (25 Nov 2026)

- **Leaves:** sessile (no stalk), ovate to lanceolate, sharply serrate, with veins sunk into the upper surface so the blade looks quilted (rugose).
- **Stems:** square, often purple-tinged.
- **Spreading:** mint spreads aggressively by creeping stolons (runners) that root at their nodes and send up new shoots, so a planting becomes a colony.
- **Flowers:** small lilac, pink or white tubular flowers in whorls (verticillasters) stacked into slender, tapering terminal spikes, with gaps between the whorls; late summer.
- **Decline:** the most common disease is mint rust (*Puccinia menthae*): dusty orange, yellow and black spots on the leaves and distorted shoots. Old stands also go leggy: lower leaves yellow and drop, and spikes go brown and seedy.
- **States as drawn:**
  - seedling: two round seed leaves, the first toothed pair above;
  - young / immature: three or four upright stems, leafy, one runner, no flowers;
  - mature: a dense colony, runners both ways, lilac spikes;
  - past / declining: taller and leggy, lower leaves gone or yellow, rust spots, brown spikes;
  - dead: dry brown stems splaying outward, a few curled brown leaves, brown spikes, dry runners.
