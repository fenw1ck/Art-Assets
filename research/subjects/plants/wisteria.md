# Wisteria (pea family, Fabaceae)

## Distinguishing features

- **Leaves:** alternate, pinnately compound, 15–35 cm long, 7–19 ovate, smooth-edged leaflets (Chinese 7–13, Japanese 9–15).
- **Climbing:** twines its stems round any support (Chinese counter-clockwise, Japanese clockwise, seen from above); no tendrils or roots. Stems grow twisted and trunk-like, up to a foot or more thick.
- **Bark:** smooth grey to grey-brown, fluted on old trunks, with lenticels (Chinese wisteria has white splotches).
- **Flowers:** fragrant pea flowers in long drooping racemes (American 5–7 cm; Chinese about 15–30 cm; Japanese up to 90 cm or more), violet to lilac, sometimes white or pink. They open from the top down, so the top of a raceme is open and pale and the tip is still in darker buds. Asian kinds bloom before the leaves are fully out.
- **Fruit:** velvety, bean-like pods 8–15 cm long (smooth in American wisteria), hanging in clusters into autumn.

## Construction order

Thick twined trunks from the frame (diagonal bands for the twist, lenticels); drooping compound leaves with 4–6 leaflet pairs and a terminal leaflet; racemes hanging straight down, flowers shrinking and darkening toward the tip; or 1–3 pods per node.

## States

- **full**, **damaged**, **cut** (as English ivy).
