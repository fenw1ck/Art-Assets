# Cucumber (Cucumis sativus)

## Distinguishing features

- **Growth architecture:** Annual creeping or climbing vine; prostrate stems can climb using tendrils.
- **Leaves:** Alternate, simple, broadly triangular-ovate to cordate blades with 3–7 angular lobes, palmate venation, toothed margins and rough/hispid surfaces; long petioles.
- **Stem and branching:** Angular, bristly stems; one simple, unbranched tendril at each leaf node, typically arising opposite the leaf. Axillary shoots may branch from nodes.
- **Flowers:** Monoecious: male flowers often in small axillary clusters; female flowers usually solitary and show a small prickly inferior ovary behind the yellow corolla.
- **Fruit:** Elongated pepo, usually green, often tuberculate or prickly when young; develops from the female ovary and hangs from its pedicel.

## Renderer construction rules

Make a trailing or trellised vine with simple tendrils (not branched squash tendrils). Attach one rough cordate-lobed leaf and a tendril at each node. Cluster some male flowers; place solitary female flowers with miniature cucumber ovaries. Fruit only follows female flowers.
