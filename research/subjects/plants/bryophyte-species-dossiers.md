# Moss and liverwort species dossiers — batch 01

Bryophytes are gametophyte-dominant. Mosses have leafy shoots; liverworts may be leafy or thalloid. Rhizoids are not vascular roots. Sporophytes remain attached to and nutritionally dependent on the gametophyte.

## *Sphagnum palustre* — blunt-leaved bog moss
- **Taxonomy:** Sphagnaceae; Sphagnales; Sphagnopsida.
- **Habit:** robust, strong-stemmed peat moss in carpets and low-to-moderate hummocks; pale green to yellow, golden/pale brown, sometimes pinkish.
- **Shoot architecture:** erect main stem with compact apical capitulum. Branch fascicles usually have four branches: two spreading and two pendent; branches long and tapering.
- **Leaves:** stem leaves to 1.7 × 1 mm; branch leaves broadly ovate, about 2.2 × 1.3 mm. Large hyaline water-storage cells alternate with narrow chlorophyllose cells; stem-leaf hyaline cells generally nonseptate and non-ornamented.
- **Reproduction:** dioicous; capsules mature mid-to-late summer; spores 24–33 µm. Capsule borne on a pseudopodium (gametophyte tissue), not a long seta.
- **Renderer:** capitulum and fascicled branch pattern are defining; show pendent branchlets. Do not use ordinary moss long-seta architecture.
- **Gaps:** local color/hummock form and species-confirmed macro photographs.
- Sources: Flora of North America https://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=1&taxon_id=200000819 ; WFO https://www.worldfloraonline.org/taxon/wfo-0001189927 .

## *Polytrichum commune* — common haircap moss
- **Taxonomy:** Polytrichaceae; Polytrichales; Polytrichopsida.
- **Habit:** dark-green, aging brown; loose or dense tall tufts, extensive patches.
- **Shoot:** stiffly erect to decumbent, typically 5–10 cm but reported 2–70 cm; mostly simple, rarely forked.
- **Leaves:** 6–8 mm, sometimes 12 mm; erect-spreading dry, spreading/recurved moist. Clasping often golden tubular sheath abruptly narrows to lanceolate blade. Strong costa ends in rough short awn. Upper surface has 5–9-cell-high photosynthetic lamellae; these are not veins.
- **Reproduction:** dioicous; stout yellowish to reddish-brown seta 5–9 cm; capsule 3–6 mm, short rectangular to cubic, four-winged, inclined horizontally; golden-yellow to brown calyptra fully covers capsule.
- **Renderer:** upright tuft, sheathing bases, narrow blades, robust seta and four-angled capsule. Lamellae belong in macro inset.
- **Gaps:** local stature and sporophyte frequency.
- Sources: Flora of North America https://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=1&taxon_id=200002665 ; Bryophyte Portal https://bryophyteportal.org/portal/taxa/index.php?clid=22&taxauthid=1&taxon=160442 .

## *Hypnum cupressiforme* — cypress-leaved plait-moss
- **Taxonomy:** Hypnaceae; Hypnales; Bryopsida. Species has substantial taxonomic variation; do not imply all material is var. *cupressiforme*.
- **Habit/shoot:** creeping pleurocarpous moss, pale/dark green to golden or yellow-brown; stems 1–8+ cm, creeping to ascending, irregularly pinnate to nearly unbranched; branches 1–3 cm. Mat spreads laterally, not as an upright tuft.
- **Leaves:** densely overlapping, 1.5–2 × 0.5–0.8 mm, ovate to oblong-lanceolate, usually falcate-secund (curved and directed to one side), tapering to slender acumen; margins recurved to plane proximally and serrulate distally; costa double, short or obscure; distinct alar cell group.
- **Reproduction:** capsules suberect to inclined, red-brown, oblong-cylindric, weakly curved; long-beaked operculum; North American flora records sporophytes summer to fall.
- **Renderer:** prostrate feather-branched mat with tiny imbricate one-sided curved leaves; no tall stem or Sphagnum head.
- **Gaps:** variety-level identity, local color and sporophyte occurrence.
- Sources: Flora of North America https://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=1&taxon_id=200002507 ; WFO https://www.worldfloraonline.org/taxon/wfo-0001148980 .

## *Marchantia polymorpha* — common liverwort
- **Taxonomy:** Marchantiaceae; Marchantiales; Marchantiopsida.
- **Habit:** robust dorsiventral thallus, typically 2–10 cm long and 7–20 mm wide; dichotomously branched with apical growth. Ventral smooth rhizoids penetrate substrate; tuberculate rhizoids run along surface.
- **Thallus:** flattened ribbon-like lobes with midrib region and apical notches. Dorsal surface has polygonal air chambers and barrel-shaped pores; ventral surface bears scales and unicellular rhizoids. Gemma cups on dorsal surface contain clonal gemmae.
- **Reproduction:** dioicous. Male antheridiophores stalked and disc-like, usually shallowly lobed; female archegoniophores stalked with radiating umbrella-like rays. Sporophytes hang beneath female rays; capsules release spores with hygroscopic elaters. Do not put both reproductive heads on one individual except a mixed-colony view.
- **Renderer:** flat dichotomously branching thallus, not leafy stems; distinguish dorsal gemma cups/air pores from ventral scales/rhizoids; male and female gametophores are separate morphs.
- **Gaps:** confirm subspecies/taxonomic concept and local phenology before fixing dates.
- Sources: USDA Forest Service https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/marpol ; Florida Museum Flora of Florida https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/67/2024/10/Flora-of-Florida-Franck-31-Oct-2024.pdf .

## *Riccia fluitans* — floating crystalwort / slender riccia
- **Taxonomy:** Ricciaceae; Marchantiales; Marchantiopsida.
- **Habit:** aquatic or seasonally stranded thalloid liverwort, usually free-floating in tangled mats below surface; often sterile and reproductive structures rarely observed.
- **Thallus:** no stem or leaves; flattened ribbon-like green strands repeatedly dichotomous, notched at tips; generally less than 1 mm wide (regional descriptions 0.5–1 mm), several cells thick. No functional roots; rhizoids few or absent in floating plants, may anchor stranded plants.
- **Reproduction:** sporangia embedded in thallus; mature structures protrude ventrally. Spores 60–90 µm, translucent brown, distal face with areolae and crenulate wing margins.
- **Renderer:** finely forked floating ribbon mat; no stems, leaves, flowers, conventional roots or visible capsules by default. Macro inset may show cross-section and rare spores.
- **Gaps:** do not invent routine sporophytes; verify local aquatic/stranded form and thallus width.
- Sources: UF IFAS https://plant-directory.ifas.ufl.edu/plant-directory/riccia-fluitans/ ; Burke Herbarium / FNA https://www.burkeherbarium.org/imagecollection/taxon.php?Taxon=Riccia+fluitans ; WA Ecology https://fortress.wa.gov/ecy/gisresources/lakes/AquaticPlantGuide/descriptions/riccia.html .

## *Frullania tamarisci* — tamarisk scalewort
- **Taxonomy:** Frullaniaceae; Porellales; Jungermanniopsida.
- **Habit:** tiny leafy liverwort, reddish brown or green-tinged; prostrate, ascending or pendent shoots appressed to bark/rock.
- **Shoot:** 0.9–1.5 mm wide; stem 180–200 µm. Branching is characteristic and lateral/ventral; not a vascular stem.
- **Leaves:** deeply bilobed. Large dorsal lobe broadly ovate, 0.8–0.9 mm; smaller ventral lobule inflated into sac/helmet, 240–300 × 100–180 µm. Orbicular underleaves 0.5 × 0.4–0.5 mm, shallowly bifid. Ocelli scattered or in short median line.
- **Reproduction:** dioicous; female inflorescences terminate short lateral branches; oblong perianth about 2.1 × 0.9 mm, narrowed to short beak.
- **Renderer:** microscopic leafy mat; paired leaf lobes, tiny inflated lobule and underleaves; macro inset essential. Never substitute large fern-like leaves.
- **Gaps:** verify local ecotype, color and substrate-specific branching.
- Sources: Burke Herbarium / FNA https://www.burkeherbarium.org/imagecollection/taxon.php?Taxon=Frullania+tamarisci ; British Bryological Society key https://www.britishbryologicalsociety.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Frullania_key.pdf .

## Cross-taxon construction rules
1. Moss sporophytes remain attached to leafy gametophytes; capsules are not flowers or fruits.
2. *Sphagnum* pseudopodium/capitulum differs from seta-bearing *Polytrichum* and *Hypnum*.
3. *Marchantia* and *Riccia* are thalloid; *Frullania* is leafy with bilobed leaves and lobules.
4. Rhizoids are anchoring filaments, not vascular roots.
5. Scale is essential; use magnified details rather than enlarging bryophytes without scale cues.
