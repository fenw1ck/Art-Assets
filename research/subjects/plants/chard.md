# Swiss chard (Beta vulgaris Cicla Group)

## Distinguishing features

- **Growth architecture:** Upright basal clump of large leaves; cultivated primarily for broad fleshy petioles and midribs.
- **Leaves:** Large alternate leaves, ovate to cordate, glossy or puckered, with broad blades; very conspicuous thick coloured petioles and midribs.
- **Stem and branching:** Short crown in the vegetative stage; leaves arise from the basal crown on long, thick petioles. A tall branched flowering stem develops in the second year.
- **Flowers:** Small greenish flowers in clusters along a long branched paniculate spike; not showy.
- **Fruit or harvest organ:** Small fruit units grouped in persistent thickened perianth bases (glomerules/seedballs).

## Renderer construction rules

Emphasize the fleshy petiole and midrib as structural elements; their colour may be white, yellow, orange, pink or red depending on cultivar. Do not create a swollen beet root. Keep the edible leaves in a basal clump; flowering is a tall second-year event.
