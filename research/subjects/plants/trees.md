# Trees

Tree habit is a growth form shared by multiple unrelated lineages. Each tree infocard must retain its botanical family and order, and its construction must be based on genus-specific branching, leaf, bark, bud, and reproductive traits.

## Broad-leaved deciduous genera

- **Betula** (Betulaceae): alternate simple leaves, commonly serrate; slender twigs with conspicuous lenticels; bark often exfoliating or marked by horizontal lenticels; male and female catkins.
- **Acer** (Sapindaceae): opposite leaves, commonly palmately lobed or compound; paired samaras; opposite branching and buds are important diagnostics.
- **Salix** (Salicaceae): alternate leaves, often narrow; flexible shoots; catkins usually appear before or with leaves; many species root readily from broken twigs.
- **Quercus** (Fagaceae): alternate leaves, variable lobing and margins; acorns borne in cupules; branching and bark differ substantially among white-oak and red-oak lineages.
- **Populus** (Salicaceae): alternate leaves, often with flattened petioles in aspens/cottonwoods; catkins; many species sucker or spread clonally.
- **Alnus** (Betulaceae): alternate simple serrate leaves; woody female strobiles (often called cones) persist; male catkins are pendulous.

## Conifers

- **Pinus** (Pinaceae): needles in fascicles, with species-specific counts; woody cones with persistent scales.
- **Picea** (Pinaceae): single stiff needles attached on persistent woody pegs; pendulous cones.
- **Tsuga** (Pinaceae): single flattened needles, often with unequal lengths and short petiole-like bases; small pendulous cones.
- **Larix** (Pinaceae): deciduous needles borne singly on new long shoots and in clusters on short shoots; small upright cones persist.

## Construction requirements

Use a hierarchical branching skeleton: trunk flare, scaffold branches, secondary branches, twigs, buds, then leaves or needles. Do not use one generic round canopy for all genera. Record opposite versus alternate branching, crown form, leaf arrangement, leaf type, venation, petiole, bark texture and age, bud placement, catkins/flowers, fruit or cones, and seasonal states. Conifers require needle insertion pattern and cone orientation/scale structure; deciduous trees require bud and twig architecture.
