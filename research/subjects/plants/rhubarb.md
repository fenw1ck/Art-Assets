# Rhubarb (Rheum rhabarbarum)

## Distinguishing features

- **Growth architecture:** Large perennial crown forming a broad basal clump; flowering stem rises well above the foliage.
- **Leaves:** Very large triangular-ovate to broadly cordate leaves with 5–7 basal veins, crinkled or wavy margins, dark upper surface and sometimes reddish underside; long stout fleshy petioles.
- **Stem and branching:** Short thick crown with leaves on individual long petioles; mature plant sends up a tall, stout, branched panicle.
- **Flowers:** Small greenish-white or yellowish-white flowers in a large branched panicle, not large solitary blooms.
- **Fruit or harvest organ:** Small winged triangular nutlet (achene) after flowering; edible harvest is the fleshy petiole, not the leaf blade or fruit.

## Renderer construction rules

Build a broad low crown of oversized blades, each on its own thick, often red petiole. Leaves are not arranged on a tall leafy stem. Flowering stage adds a central tall panicle. Keep the petiole scale conspicuously thicker than ordinary leaf stalks.
