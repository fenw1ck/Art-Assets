# Fig (Ficus carica)

## Distinguishing features

- **Growth architecture:** Broad branching deciduous tree or large shrub, often multi-stemmed; branches contain milky latex.
- **Leaves:** Large alternate simple leaves, usually 3–5 deep palm-shaped lobes, rough upper surface, long petiole and palmate venation.
- **Stem and branching:** Woody branching framework with stout shoots and broad irregular canopy. Leaves and syconia occur at nodes along current growth.
- **Flowers:** Flowers are tiny and enclosed inside a hollow fleshy receptacle (syconium); no showy external petals are visible.
- **Fruit:** The fig is a syconium, an enclosed inflorescence that matures as a fleshy hollow structure, often pear-shaped, borne singly or in pairs in leaf axils.

## Renderer construction rules

Use large rough deeply lobed leaves with palmate veins. Place figs in leaf axils on shoots, not as berries in exposed clusters. Never draw visible daisy-like flowers; the flowers are inside the syconium. Branching is woody and broad.
