# Garden nasturtium (*Tropaeolum majus*)

## Distinguishing features
- Strong-growing annual, trailing or scrambling; stems spill over container edges or climb only when supported. Do not give it tomato-like recursive suckers or pea-like tendrils.
- Leaves are round, shield-like (peltate), with the petiole attaching near the centre of the blade. Veins radiate from that attachment; margins are softly wavy.
- Flowers are wide-mouthed/funnel-shaped, with five long-clawed petals and a conspicuous nectar spur behind the flower. Flowers may be single or double depending on cultivar.
- Flowers appear singly from leaf axils along the trailing stems.

## Construction rules
1. Build a small number of long, flexible primary stems that arc outward and down; add occasional side shoots, not a recursively repeating tomato shoot.
2. Place alternate long-petioled peltate leaves along stems. Each blade is circular with radiating veins; vary diameter, angle and overlap.
3. Place axillary flower pedicels independently at nodes; seed each branch with 0–5 open flowers in the mature form. Buds remain distinct from open flowers.
4. Immature: no open flowers, with occasional buds only. Mature: dense foliage, variable flower count and colour. Past flowering: reduce open flowers gradually from about 15 toward 8 while retaining most leaves and stems.
5. Provide 15 deterministic mature forms through seed variation in stem paths, leaf sizes, petiole angles, branching and flower placement. Variation must not change the trailing species architecture.
