# Spinach (Spinacia oleracea)

## Distinguishing features

- **Growth architecture:** Cool-season annual or biennial herb; basal rosette of leaves, then an erect branched seed stalk.
- **Leaves:** Alternate simple leaves in a ground-level rosette; lower leaves long-petiolate, ovate to triangular or arrow-shaped, smooth or savoyed; basal lobes may be hastate.
- **Stem and branching:** Vegetative stem is very short. Bolting produces a tall, angular, branching inflorescence axis with reduced upper leaves. Species is usually dioecious.
- **Flowers:** Tiny green flowers without showy petals; male flowers form clusters in spikes or leafy panicles, female flowers are sessile axillary clusters.
- **Fruit or harvest organ:** Small dry fruit enclosed by persistent hardened female perianth; fruit clusters may bear spines in some forms.

## Renderer construction rules

Keep the edible stage as a low rosette of petiolate leaves. For mature seed-stage reference, add a separate upright branched flowering stalk. Do not render showy blossoms; male and female flower structures are minute and green.
