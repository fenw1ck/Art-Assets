# Fern and fern-allies species dossiers — batch 01

Ferns are constructed from rhizomes, fronds, rachises, pinnae/pinnules and sori; these must not be treated as ordinary flowering-plant leaves or flowers. *Equisetum* is a fern ally (a horsetail), not a true fern, and has jointed stems and strobili rather than fronds.

## *Osmunda regalis* — royal fern
- **Taxonomy:** Osmundaceae; Osmundales; Polypodiopsida.
- **Habit:** large deciduous wetland fern with erect/suberect rhizome, persistent winged stipe bases and black fibrous roots; fronds tufted.
- **Fronds:** erect, 2-pinnate, up to about 1 m in North American treatments (larger in some regional sources); sterile and fertile portions dimorphic. Sterile pinnae broad with oblong pinnules; fertile pinnae/pinnules are strongly contracted and borne toward the frond apex.
- **Sori/reproduction:** sporangia in dense clusters on contracted terminal fertile segments, not in round discrete sori with indusia. Sporangia exposed on both surfaces of fertile pinnae.
- **Renderer:** construct a large vase-like clump of arching bipinnate fronds; fertile fronds have a distinct brown/green bead-like or clustered apical portion. Do not cover the entire underside of sterile pinnae with sori.
- **Gaps:** local frond dimensions and fertility frequency; regional taxonomic concept (American plants sometimes treated as var. *spectabilis*).
- Sources: WFO https://www.worldfloraonline.org/taxon/wfo-0001109067 ; Kew POWO https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:305721-2/general-information .

## *Dryopteris filix-mas* — male fern
- **Taxonomy:** Dryopteridaceae; Polypodiales; Polypodiopsida.
- **Habit:** stout rhizomatous woodland fern; fronds monomorphic, deciduous, 28–120 cm × 10–30 cm.
- **Fronds:** blade ovate-lanceolate, pinnate-pinnatifid to 2-pinnate at base, firm but not leathery; pinnae lanceolate and held approximately in plane of blade; basal pinnae reduced. Petiole less than one-quarter frond length, with two distinct scale types: broad brown scales and hairlike scales.
- **Sori:** round sori midway between segment midvein and margin; indusia present and eglandular.
- **Renderer:** upright crown of arching once-to-twice divided fronds, tapering at both ends; show the two scale morphologies in a stipe inset and medial sori on pinna segments. Avoid spiny pinna margins.
- **Gaps:** North American *D. filix-mas* is taxonomically complex and may comprise closely related taxa; confirm local specimen identity.
- Sources: Flora of North America https://efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=1&taxon_id=200004423 ; Burke Herbarium https://burkeherbarium.org/imagecollection/taxon.php?Taxon=Dryopteris+filix-mas .

## *Polystichum munitum* — western sword fern
- **Taxonomy:** Dryopteridaceae; Polypodiales; Polypodiopsida.
- **Habit:** evergreen, arching crown from erect/ascending scaly stem; old fronds may remain attached. Fronds commonly 50–180 cm, with regional/site range wider.
- **Fronds:** linear-lanceolate, once-pinnate, slightly narrowed at base; petiole 1/8–1/4 frond length, densely covered with dark red-brown scales. Pinnae narrowly lanceolate, straight to falcate, not overlapping, with a pronounced acroscopic auricle and ascending spiny teeth; shade pinnae often lie in one plane, sun pinnae may twist.
- **Sori:** round sori in one or several rows on pinnae; each with ciliate indusium.
- **Renderer:** show a broad arching once-pinnate frond with many close but non-overlapping sword-shaped pinnae, scaly stipe and round indusiate sori. Preserve spiny teeth and basal auricles.
- **Gaps:** frond length varies markedly with site; local size and sori row arrangement need reference specimens.
- Sources: WFO/FNA https://www.worldfloraonline.org/taxon/wfo-0001109274 ; USDA Forest Service FEIS https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/polmun .

## *Pteridium aquilinum* — bracken fern
- **Taxonomy:** Dennstaedtiaceae; Polypodiales; Polypodiopsida.
- **Habit:** extensive clonal perennial from long-creeping rhizomes; fronds arise singly at intervals rather than as a compact crown.
- **Fronds:** erect, typically 0.5–1.5 m tall; stout brown stipe; blade broadly triangular/deltate, 3–4-pinnate, often with widely spreading lower pinnae. Ultimate segments deeply pinnatifid; lower surface may be hairy depending on form.
- **Sori:** continuous or nearly continuous marginal/submarginal line along ultimate segments; false indusium formed by inrolled leaf margin plus inner membranous indusial tissue, not discrete round sori.
- **Renderer:** build widely spaced fronds from a long horizontal rhizome, each with a large triangular multi-divided blade. Show marginal sori as continuous lines on segment edges, not dots.
- **Gaps:** *Pteridium aquilinum* is a species complex with regional taxa and variable indument; use regional flora to refine.
- Sources: SANBI species morphology https://biodiversityadvisor.sanbi.org/search/detail/2df7e7a8-fc57-470a-ac6f-08483399ec00 ; Flora of North America overview and taxonomic treatments should be consulted for regional infraspecific concept.

## *Adiantum pedatum* — northern maidenhair fern
- **Taxonomy:** Pteridaceae; Polypodiales; Polypodiopsida.
- **Habit:** short-creeping rhizome, bronzy/deep-yellow scales; lax-arching fronds 40–75 cm, closely spaced.
- **Fronds:** petiole slender, glabrous and dark chestnut/purple; blade fan-shaped and pseudopedate, 15–30 × 15–35 cm. Rachis forks near the top into two arching branches; proximal pinnae more divided, ultimate segments oblong and asymmetrical with rounded shallow lobes.
- **Sori:** transversely oblong indusia 1–3 mm borne in shallow sinuses along the upper/acroscopic segment margins; false indusium is formed by reflexed margin.
- **Renderer:** signature dark polished forked rachis and fan-shaped architecture; tiny delicate pinnules on curved branches. Sori sit at lobe margins, not as round dots near midrib.
- **Gaps:** *A. pedatum* complex includes distinct western *A. aleuticum*; ensure the card depicts the eastern species concept and do not conflate.
- Sources: Flora of North America https://efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=1&taxon_id=200003542 ; University of Saskatchewan fern guide https://gardening.usask.ca/documents/outside-source-pdf/Ferns_and_Fern_Allies_of_Canada.pdf .

## *Polypodium vulgare* — common polypody
- **Taxonomy:** Polypodiaceae; Polypodiales; Polypodiopsida.
- **Habit:** creeping, scaly rhizome 3–4 mm diameter; fronds spaced apart along rhizome rather than forming a tight crown.
- **Fronds:** stipe straw-colored, 5–10 cm; blade pinnatifid to pinnatisect, oblong-lanceolate, about 10–20 × 5–7 cm; 12–15 pairs of oblique lanceolate segments, margins toothed; fronds persistent.
- **Sori:** round sori medial or slightly closer to costa, typically naked (without indusia) and orange-yellow when mature.
- **Renderer:** show creeping rhizome with discrete fronds emerging at intervals; simple pinnatifid blade with broad lobes and round sori in rows on underside. Do not add indusia.
- **Gaps:** species complex and local frond/sorus color variation need specimen-level confirmation.
- Sources: WFO / Flora of China description https://www.worldfloraonline.org/taxon/wfo-0001107395 ; Flora of North America regional key should be used to confirm local taxonomic concept.

## *Equisetum arvense* — field horsetail
- **Taxonomy:** Equisetaceae; Equisetales; Polypodiopsida (fern ally).
- **Habit:** rhizomatous perennial with strongly dimorphic aerial shoots.
- **Shoots:** fertile shoots appear first, brownish to whitish, unbranched, ephemeral, 5–30 cm tall, with terminal blunt strobilus 1–3.5 cm. Sterile green shoots later, 5–60 cm (rarely 1 m), hollow and jointed, with up to about 20 whorls of slender ascending branches; central cavity about one-third to two-thirds stem diameter. Leaves are tiny scale-like teeth fused into sheaths at nodes; stems ridged and silicified.
- **Reproduction:** spores produced in terminal strobili on separate fertile shoots; no flowers, seeds, sori or fern fronds.
- **Renderer:** show two separate seasonal shoot forms: unbranched pale fertile stems with terminal cone, and green jointed sterile stems with whorled branches. Keep nodal sheaths and hollow internodes visible in macro inset.
- **Gaps:** regional emergence timing and shoot proportions vary; avoid showing fertile and sterile structures on one continuous stem.
- Sources: USDA Forest Service FEIS https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/equarv ; Burke Herbarium / Flora of North America https://www.burkeherbarium.org/imagecollection/taxon.php?Taxon=Equisetum+arvense .

## Cross-taxon construction rules
1. Fronds are divided into pinnae and pinnules; preserve the actual order and branching depth for each taxon.
2. Sori may be discrete, marginal, or absent from sterile fronds; indusia may be present or absent. Never use a generic dotted underside.
3. *Osmunda* fertile pinnae are contracted and terminal; *Pteridium* sori form marginal lines; *Adiantum* sori sit in marginal sinuses; *Polypodium* sori are naked and medial.
4. *Equisetum* is not a frond-bearing fern: render jointed stems and dimorphic fertile/sterile shoots.
