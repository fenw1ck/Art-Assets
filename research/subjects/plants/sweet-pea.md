# Sweet pea (*Lathyrus odoratus*)

## Distinguishing features
- Annual climbing/scrambling vine, commonly reaching about 2 m with support; dwarf cultivars are more compact.
- Stems have conspicuous narrow wings.
- Leaves are compound, typically with one pair of broad leaflets; the terminal portion is modified into branching tendrils that grasp support.
- Flower stalks arise from leaf axils and carry short racemes, usually 1–4 pea-shaped flowers.
- Each flower is papilionaceous: a broad banner (standard), two lateral wings and a lower keel formed from paired petals. Bilateral, not radial.
- Flower colours span white, pink, red, purple and bicolours.

## Construction rules
1. Draw a climbing/scrambling stem with visible nodes and narrow stem wings. Support/tendril attachment determines the growth direction.
2. At alternate nodes, place a paired leaflet set and a terminal tendril; tendrils curl around nearby supports.
3. Place axillary peduncles separate from the tendril-bearing leaf, each carrying 1–4 flowers in a raceme.
4. Flowers use banner, wings and keel geometry; avoid generic radial-petal flowers.
5. Immature: growing shoots, leaves and tendrils, no open flowers. Mature: climbing stems with staggered axillary racemes. Past flowering: fewer open corollas and spent pedicels; tendrils and stems remain.
6. Seed variation changes internode length, branch count, tendril curl and raceme size, but not the species architecture.
