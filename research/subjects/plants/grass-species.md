# Grass species morphology

Grass infocards must record the sheath, ligule, blade, culm nodes/internodes and inflorescence separately. Grass blades are alternate and two-ranked on the culm; they are not petioled broad leaves. The inflorescence is assembled from spikelets and florets.

## Big bluestem — *Andropogon gerardii*

- Tall warm-season perennial bunchgrass, commonly 6–9 feet, spreading by short rhizomes.
- Blades are rolled in the bud; sheaths are round to somewhat flattened, open and often hairy or purplish at base; ligule is small and membranous.
- Inflorescence typically has several fingerlike racemes; exact species-level spikelet details need a dedicated flora source.
- **Reference:** USDA NRCS, [Native Grasses & Plants](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2022-09/NRCS%20Native%20Grasses%20%26%20Plants.pdf), supports habit, height, sheath, blade and ligule characters.

## Little bluestem — *Schizachyrium scoparium*

- Warm-season perennial bunchgrass, commonly 2–4 feet; short rhizomes may occur.
- Blades are folded in the bud and folded along the midrib; sheaths are flattened, open and often purplish at base; ligule is small and membranous.
- **Reference:** USDA NRCS, [Native Grasses & Plants](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2022-09/NRCS%20Native%20Grasses%20%26%20Plants.pdf), supports growth form and diagnostic vegetative traits. Inflorescence details remain open pending a species flora.

## Switchgrass — *Panicum virgatum*

- Warm-season perennial, 3–6 feet, spreading by rhizomes and forming sod or loose colonies depending on cultivar and site.
- Blades are rolled in the bud and flat; sheaths are rounded and open; the ligule is prominent and membranous.
- The species bears an open terminal panicle of spikelets; panicle dimensions and openness vary by genotype and habitat.
- **Reference:** USDA NRCS, [Native Grasses & Plants](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2022-09/NRCS%20Native%20Grasses%20%26%20Plants.pdf), supports habit, blade, sheath and ligule characters. Panicle variability requires further species/cultivar-level documentation.

## Construction rules

- Preserve the diagnostic ligule and sheath; do not depict grass blades attached by petioles.
- Use culm nodes and internodes as the stem framework.
- Keep species-specific ligule, sheath hairiness, blade vernation and inflorescence form distinct.
- These three profiles use a regional NRCS identification guide. Add a second taxon-specific flora or herbarium source before marking all flower traits verified.
