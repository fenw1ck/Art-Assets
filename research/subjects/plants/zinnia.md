# Common zinnia (*Zinnia elegans*)

## Distinguishing features
- Bushy annual with upright, well-branched stems.
- Simple opposite leaves, often sessile or nearly so, ovate to lanceolate, with rough/bristly texture.
- Solitary terminal composite flower heads on branch tips. Heads can be single, semi-double or fully double; the ray florets surround a central disc, which may be partly or wholly obscured in double cultivars.
- Strong colour variation across cultivars; flower-head construction remains composite.

## Construction rules
1. Build an upright, bushy framework with opposite leaves and clear branch forks.
2. Place one terminal head at each flowering shoot tip; avoid axillary clusters of many small blooms.
3. Model a composite head with layered rays and a central disc; allow cultivar parameters to change ray density.
4. Immature: opposite foliage and terminal buds. Mature: solitary terminal heads at branch tips. Past flowering: spent heads remain at the tips, with ray colour fading and foliage retained.
5. Seed branch density, leaf size, head size and cultivar colour while preserving opposite leaves and terminal heads.
