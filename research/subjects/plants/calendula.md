# Calendula (*Calendula officinalis*)

## Distinguishing features
- Annual or short-lived herb; erect to lax/sprawling stems with branching.
- Simple, alternate, mostly sessile leaves; lower blades tend toward oblanceolate/spatulate, upper leaves narrower and may clasp the stem. Margins are entire to minutely toothed; foliage can be hairy/glandular.
- Flowering units are composite heads, not single many-petalled flowers: a ring or several rows of strap-shaped ray florets around a central disc of tubular florets.
- Heads are solitary at branch tips; additional heads form as axillary branches extend.
- Rays are yellow to orange, sometimes with dark central discs; cultivated doubles increase ray density but retain the composite head structure.

## Construction rules
1. Build a branching, upright-to-open stem framework; do not use a trailing vine or dense rosette.
2. Place alternate simple leaves along the stems. Use broader lower leaves and narrower upper leaves; keep them sessile or very shortly stalked.
3. Place one composite head at the end of each flowering branch. Draw rays around a distinct disc; never model each ray as an independent flower.
4. Immature: foliage and terminal buds, no open heads. Mature: several terminal heads at staggered heights. Past flowering: drooping/fading heads and reduced ray colour; keep the branch and leaves.
5. Use seeded differences in branch count, stem angle, leaf width and head size. Keep the plant's upright branching habit stable.
