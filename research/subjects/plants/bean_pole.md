# Pole bean (Phaseolus vulgaris (pole / climbing growth-habit cultivars))

## Distinguishing features

- **Growth architecture:** Indeterminate twining climber; long flexible stems wind around a support.
- **Leaves:** Alternate, long-petioled trifoliate leaves; three broad ovate leaflets with entire margins and pinnate venation.
- **Stem and branching:** A slender twining main stem elongates upward around a stake or trellis; side branches emerge from nodes. Climbing is by stem twining, not pea-like terminal tendrils.
- **Flowers:** Axillary or terminal racemes bearing paired or solitary papilionaceous flowers, typically white through pink or purple.
- **Fruit:** Elongated pods hang from flower-bearing nodes; pods may be green, yellow, purple, round or flat depending on cultivar.

## Renderer construction rules

Construct one or more long spiralling stems around a visible support. Keep leaf insertion alternate and trifoliate. Place racemes at leaf axils; let pods hang from those same reproductive nodes. Do not substitute tendrils for the twining stem.
