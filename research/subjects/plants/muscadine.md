# Muscadine grape (*Vitis rotundifolia*)

## Distinguishing features

- **Leaves:** nearly round to heart-shaped, 10–15 cm, coarse blunt teeth of similar size all round, usually unlobed; shiny green above, yellowish green below. Smaller and rounder than other wild grapes.
- **Tendrils:** simple (unforked), opposite the leaves. Other wild grapes have forked tendrils: this is the surest way to tell a muscadine.
- **Bark:** smooth, not peeling, greenish brown to grey with lenticels; old trunks develop vertical grooves.
- **Fruit:** large (1.3–2.5 cm), thick-skinned berries, singly or a few together in loose small clusters (not bunches); dark purple to black, or bronze ('Scuppernong'); green when unripe. Many wild vines are male and bear none.

## Construction order

Stems from the frame; round toothed leaves at each node; coiled simple tendrils at about half the nodes; berries in loose groups of 3–7 on some doors (purple, or bronze on about one door in three).

## States

- **full**, **damaged**, **cut** (as English ivy).
