# Summer squash / zucchini (Cucurbita pepo (summer-squash cultivars))

## Distinguishing features

- **Growth architecture:** Vining or bush-form annual; many zucchini cultivars are compact with short internodes, while trailing forms produce long runners.
- **Leaves:** Large alternate simple leaves, broadly ovate to triangular-cordate, palmately veined, commonly 5–7 lobed, toothed and rough-hairy; long stout petioles.
- **Stem and branching:** Angular, bristly stems; axillary tendrils commonly branched and coiling. Bush cultivars have short internodes and limited runners; vining cultivars trail.
- **Flowers:** Large solitary yellow funnel/bell-shaped flowers in leaf axils. Separate male and female flowers occur on the same plant; female flowers have a conspicuous inferior ovary behind the corolla.
- **Fruit:** Pepo develops directly behind the female flower; zucchini is elongated and harvested immature. Fruit stalk is firm and angular.

## Renderer construction rules

Determine bush versus vining habit before construction. Use coarse, lobed leaves on long petioles and coiling branched tendrils at nodes. Show male flowers on longer stalks and female flowers with a miniature zucchini-shaped ovary. Do not put fruit at male flowers.
