# Ferns and fern allies

Fern fronds are megaphylls that commonly emerge by circinate vernation from a rhizome or crown. The plant body is a sporophyte; spores are produced in sporangia, often grouped into sori. Horsetails (*Equisetum*) are fern allies, not true ferns, and require a separate shoot construction.

## Representative architectures

- **Osmunda** (Osmundaceae): robust terrestrial ferns; fronds often large and bipinnate, with fertile pinnae or portions differentiated from sterile tissue in some species.
- **Dryopteris** (Dryopteridaceae): terrestrial rhizomatous ferns; blades commonly one- to three-pinnate; round sori occur on the lower blade, often with kidney-shaped indusia.
- **Polystichum** (Dryopteridaceae): evergreen or semi-evergreen fronds, commonly once-pinnate with toothed pinnae; round sori typically have peltate indusia.
- **Pteridium** (Dennstaedtiaceae): extensive creeping rhizomes; large, triangular, highly divided fronds; sori form a continuous marginal line protected by false and true indusial tissue.
- **Adiantum** (Pteridaceae): dark, wiry stipes and rachises; fan-shaped pinnules; sori borne along reflexed marginal lobes.
- **Polypodium** (Polypodiaceae): creeping rhizomes; simple to pinnatifid fronds; round sori generally lack indusia.
- **Equisetum** (Equisetaceae): jointed, ribbed, hollow aerial stems with whorled branches and reduced leaves fused into sheaths; terminal strobili bear spores. Do not draw as a pinnate fern.

## Construction requirements

Build the rhizome/crown first, then frond emergence, stipe, rachis, pinnae and pinnules. Keep fertile and sterile fronds distinct where dimorphic. Sori placement and indusium shape are species- and family-level traits; include magnified underside insets. Show fiddleheads only on young emerging fronds, not as a permanent mature-state feature.
