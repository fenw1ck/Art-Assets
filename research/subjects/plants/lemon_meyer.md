# Meyer lemon (Citrus × meyeri)

## Distinguishing features

- **Growth architecture:** Small evergreen broadleaf tree or shrub with compact, bushy to spreading crown; branches may bear short spines.
- **Leaves:** Alternate simple elliptic to ovate glossy aromatic leaves; citrus leaves often have a winged petiole, though Meyer lemon cultivar descriptions report reduced or absent wings.
- **Stem and branching:** Woody branching stems, sometimes spiny; new flush may be reddish before turning green. Leaves persist through the year.
- **Flowers:** Fragrant white flowers, often with purplish exterior, solitary or in small axillary clusters; buds may be reddish.
- **Fruit:** Broadly ovoid yellow to yellow-orange citrus hesperidium with aromatic rind; fruits may occur with flowers and buds simultaneously.

## Renderer construction rules

Render an evergreen woody shrub with glossy alternate leaves and a fine branch framework. Treat petiole winging as a cultivar-level variable, not a guaranteed large wing. Put fragrant white/purple flowers in leaf axils and oval yellow fruit on short stalks; multiple stages can coexist.
