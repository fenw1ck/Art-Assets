# English ivy (*Hedera helix*, ivy family Araliaceae)

## Distinguishing features

- **Evergreen, two stages.** The climbing (juvenile) stage carries leaves with 3–5 triangular lobes and a heart-shaped base. The adult stage turns shrubby, stops climbing, and carries unlobed, diamond to oval leaves; only adult stems flower.
- **Leaves:** alternate, thick and waxy, glossy dark green with paler, whitish veins, lighter underneath, about 5–10 cm long.
- **Climbing:** short aerial rootlets along the stems grip any rough surface; it rarely branches.
- **Stems:** green when young, then light brown, slightly rough and finely scaly, with rootlets at the nodes; old stems can be very thick.
- **Flowers:** small (3–5 mm), greenish-white to yellow-green, in round umbels, in autumn.
- **Fruit:** round black (blue-black) drupes, under about 1 cm, in umbels.

## Construction order

Stems from the frame (rootlets on one side), leaves at every node alternating sides, blades turned partly downward; flowering doors use the adult leaf shape on part of the leaves and add umbels.

## States

- **full**, **damaged** (cut stems show pale wood; leaves near the cut wilt yellow-brown), **cut** (stubs at the frame, a heap of cut stems and wilting leaves at the foot).
