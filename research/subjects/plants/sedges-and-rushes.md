# Sedges and rushes

Sedges (Cyperaceae) and rushes (Juncaceae) are separate families. Do not use a single generic grass construction for both.

## Sedges — Cyperaceae

- Culms are commonly solid and triangular in cross-section, though round or compressed culms occur.
- Leaves are commonly three-ranked, with closed sheaths; blades are linear with parallel venation.
- Flowers are reduced and grouped into spikelets with scales. In *Carex*, each pistillate flower is enclosed by a perigynium, a diagnostic sac-like structure.
- *Cyperus* commonly has a terminal cluster of spikelets subtended by leaf-like involucral bracts; spikelet scales are often two-ranked.
- *Eriophorum* has conspicuous elongate perianth bristles that become cottony tufts at fruiting.
- *Schoenoplectus* has stout, usually leafless or sparsely leafy culms and terminal or apparently lateral spikelet clusters; the apparent lateral position is often caused by a bract that continues the culm axis.

## Rushes — Juncaceae

- Culms may be round or flattened; leaves are often basal or cauline and may be cylindrical, channelled, or flat.
- *Juncus* commonly has pithy or septate cylindrical stems and reduced leaves; the inflorescence can appear lateral because a bract continues the stem.
- *Luzula* has flat grass-like leaves, often with conspicuous marginal hairs, and cymose inflorescences.
- Flowers are small, with tepals rather than showy petals; fruits are capsules.

## Construction requirements

Record culm cross-section (solid/hollow; round/triangular/compressed), leaf rank and sheath, ligule, inflorescence architecture, spikelet scale arrangement, perigynium or perianth bristles, and achene/capsule shape. Cross-section should be represented in an inset rather than inferred from the outer silhouette.
