# Sedge and rush species dossiers — batch 01

Cyperaceae and Juncaceae require separate construction rules. Sedges commonly have solid, often angled culms and spikelet/scale/perigynium structures; rushes commonly have round culms, reduced leaves or sheaths, and capsule-bearing inflorescences. The inflorescence's apparent position and bract arrangement are diagnostic.

## *Carex aquatilis* — water sedge
- **Taxonomy:** Cyperaceae; Cyperales/Poales (modern placement Poales); Carex.
- **Habit:** rhizomatous perennial, usually not densely cespitose; wet meadows, swamps, pond/lake shores and streambanks.
- **Culms/leaves:** culms 20–150 cm; erect, angled, exceeding leaves. Basal sheaths red-brown/brown; blades flat, long-tapering, light to glaucous green, 2.5–8 mm wide, papillose on both surfaces.
- **Inflorescence:** erect spikes; proximal 2–6 spikes pistillate, terminal 1–4 spikes staminate; lowest bract leaflike and longer than inflorescence. Pistillate perigynia ascending, pale brown, ellipsoid/obovoid, flattened, 2–3.6 × 1.3–2.3 mm, papillose, beak to 0.2 mm; achenes lenticular/glossy; two stigmas.
- **Renderer:** show a sedge culm with distinct terminal staminate spikes above lateral/basal pistillate spikes; retain long bract, basal red-brown sheaths and rhizomatous habit. Perigynia need a close-up; do not substitute grass florets.
- **Gaps:** the species is variable and four intergrading North American varieties are recognized; exact variety and local perigynium traits require specimen confirmation.
- Sources: Flora of North America https://efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=1&taxon_id=242357042 ; USDA Forest Service field key https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/download/6196.pdf .

## *Cyperus esculentus* — yellow nutsedge
- **Taxonomy:** Cyperaceae; Poales; Cyperus.
- **Habit:** perennial, stoloniferous sedge; soft, flexible stolons 2–8(–15) cm bear tubers 3–11 mm diameter.
- **Culms/leaves:** culms trigonous, 15–60(–100) cm × 0.6–3.4 mm, glabrous. Leaves 3–7, basal, flat to V-shaped/flanged V-shaped, (6–)20–40(–80) cm × 2–4(–6.5) mm.
- **Inflorescence:** usually simple anthela with 3–10 primary rays, sometimes branching; 3–7 leaflike involucral bracts, lowest 5–30 cm; spikelets in loose clusters, narrow linear-lanceoloid, 5–20(–55) mm × 1.2–2(–3) mm, yellowish to dark brown; nutlet shiny grey, trigonous, 1.3–2 mm.
- **Renderer:** show basal leaf fan, triangular culm, open umbrella-like anthela and below-ground stolons ending in distinct tubers. Tubers are not roots or bulbs at the crown.
- **Gaps:** tuber number/size and shoot architecture vary with soil and cultivation; local ecotype and flowering stage need reference.
- Sources: Flora of North America https://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=1&taxon_id=220003732 ; Kew Plants of the World Online https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:300668-2/general-information .

## *Juncus effusus* — soft rush
- **Taxonomy:** Juncaceae; Juncales/Poales (modern placement Poales); Juncus.
- **Habit:** densely tufted perennial from short-branched rhizomes, often forming clumps.
- **Culms/leaves:** erect, smooth, cylindrical, 25–90 cm or taller, 1–3(–4) mm diameter, with continuous pith. True leaf blades absent; basal cataphylls/sheaths reddish to chestnut brown, closely embracing culms.
- **Inflorescence:** apparently lateral, many-flowered compound dichasium; a long erect terete involucral bract appears to continue the culm beyond the inflorescence. Tepals pale tan to brown, 1.9–3.5 mm; usually three stamens; capsule greenish tan to brown, three-loculed, 1.5–3.2 mm; seeds 0.4–0.5 mm.
- **Renderer:** cylindrical pithy green culms with brown basal sheaths; inflorescence emerges from the side below a culm-like bract, not at a terminal grass-like head. No broad leaf blades.
- **Gaps:** *J. effusus* is a complex with regional subspecies; confirm subspecific identity before fixing fine characters or range.
- Sources: Burke Herbarium / Flora of North America https://www.burkeherbarium.org/imagecollection/taxon.php?Taxon=Juncus+effusus ; World Flora Online https://www.worldfloraonline.org/taxon/wfo-0000776350 .

## *Eriophorum vaginatum* — tussock cottongrass
- **Taxonomy:** Cyperaceae; Poales; Eriophorum.
- **Habit:** densely cespitose tussock-forming perennial of bogs, tundra, wet meadows and peaty soils; short rhizomes/tillers; culms 10–60 cm.
- **Culms/leaves:** brown basal sheaths; 1–3 bladeless sheaths per culm; basal blades filiform, trigonous and no more than about 1 mm wide, clustered at base.
- **Inflorescence:** one solitary erect terminal spikelet; 10–20 mm in flower, up to 50 mm in fruit. More than 10 white (rarely reddish/brown) perianth bristles, 10–18 mm, become conspicuous cottony tufts as fruit matures; achenes 1.9–3.5 mm.
- **Renderer:** dense tussock with narrow basal leaves and one terminal cottony head per flowering culm. The cotton is perianth bristles around fruit, not a multi-spikelet plume.
- **Gaps:** recognized subspecies overlap morphologically; do not encode a subspecies without specimen evidence. Local tussock height and fruiting season vary.
- Sources: Flora of North America https://efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=1&taxon_id=200026766 ; USDA Forest Service FEIS https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/erivag .

## *Schoenoplectus tabernaemontani* — softstem bulrush
- **Taxonomy:** Cyperaceae; Poales; Schoenoplectus.
- **Habit:** robust rhizomatous emergent perennial, forming dense colonies; horizontal rhizomes 3–10 mm diameter.
- **Culms/leaves:** smooth, soft, cylindrical culms 0.5–3 m × 2–10 mm; 3–4 basal leaf sheaths, usually only 1–2 short blades; sheaths membranous and often splitting into fibers.
- **Inflorescence:** apparently lateral, open to compact anthela, 2–4 times branched; principal bract erect, culm-like, 1–8 cm; 15–200 spikelets, solitary or clustered, 3–17 × 2.5–4 mm. Six brown retrorsely barbed perianth bristles; achenes plano-convex, 1.5–2.8 mm.
- **Renderer:** cylindrical pithy culms with few basal sheaths and one erect culm-like bract; open lateral umbel of brown spikelets. Do not use triangular culms or a dense terminal plume.
- **Gaps:** culm height, bract length and inflorescence openness vary by habitat; check regional forms.
- Sources: Flora of North America https://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=1&taxon_id=242357954 ; Burke Herbarium https://burkeherbarium.org/imagecollection/taxon.php?Taxon=Schoenoplectus+tabernaemontani .

## *Luzula multiflora* — common wood-rush
- **Taxonomy:** Juncaceae; Juncales/Poales (modern placement Poales); Luzula.
- **Habit:** densely to loosely tufted perennial; culms 10–40 cm.
- **Leaves:** basal blades 3.5–12 cm × 2–6 mm; cauline leaves similar, not overlapping and equaling or exceeding inflorescence. Margins have characteristic long pale hairs in the genus.
- **Inflorescence/reproduction:** 3–16 mostly cylindrical glomerules, each with 8–16 flowers; one or two nearly sessile, others on evident peduncles; branches usually straight and erect. Tepals pale brown to chestnut/blackish, 2–4 mm, six pointed tepals; globose capsule pale to dark, shorter than to about equaling tepals; seeds 1.1–1.7 mm.
- **Renderer:** tufted grasslike leaves with visibly hairy margins; multiple discrete flower clusters on unequal peduncles, not one dense terminal spike. Capsules globose and enclosed by persistent tepals.
- **Gaps:** subspecies/variety treatments and local cluster counts vary; confirm regional taxonomic treatment.
- Sources: Burke Herbarium / Flora of North America https://burkeherbarium.org/imagecollection/taxon.php?Taxon=Luzula+multiflora ; World Flora Online https://www.worldfloraonline.org/taxon/wfo-0000 .

## Cross-taxon construction rules
1. Do not treat all graminoids as grasses: *Carex*, *Cyperus*, *Eriophorum*, and *Schoenoplectus* have sedge spikelets; *Juncus* and *Luzula* are rushes.
2. *Carex* perigynia enclose the achene and must be distinct from glumes and scales.
3. *Cyperus esculentus* has stolons terminating in tubers; this underground architecture is essential.
4. *Juncus effusus* has leafless, pith-filled round culms and a pseudolateral inflorescence.
5. *Eriophorum vaginatum* has a solitary terminal spikelet and cottony perianth bristles.
