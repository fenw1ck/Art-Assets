# Aquatic species dossiers — batch 01

These are species-level refinements, not generic aquatic templates. Measurements are reported as ranges where the cited flora or agency profile supplies them. Habitat-dependent dimensions must not be treated as fixed drawing proportions.

## *Nymphaea odorata* — American white water lily

- **Taxonomy:** Nymphaeaceae; Nymphaeales; accepted name *Nymphaea odorata* Aiton.
- **Habit and attachment:** perennial rooted in sediment by a thick, creeping, branched rhizome; rhizomes are repent and stolons are absent. Adventitious roots arise below leaf bases.
- **Leaf construction:** floating, nearly orbicular blades with a deep basal cleft; entire margins; radiating primary veins without a central reticulate web. Upper surface green and waxy; lower surface may be purplish. Blade diameter is variable; the cited flora reports 5–40 cm, with a typical mature leaf about 6–8 inches across in regional extension material. Petioles are long, terete and flexible; lengths vary with water depth and can reach approximately 2 m.
- **Reproductive construction:** solitary, long-pedunculate, fragrant flowers at the water surface or slightly above it; 4 sepals; typically 17–43 white petals (rarely pink); numerous yellow stamens. Flower opens and closes diurnally over successive days. After flowering, the peduncle coils and draws the berry-like capsule underwater.
- **Renderer constraints:** keep the rhizome below the waterline; leaf blades lie flat at the surface, with a visible basal cleft; flower stalk is separate from leaf petioles; do not render the flower as an upright emergent lotus.
- **Remaining gap:** verify regional subspecies/variety and seasonal phenology before fixing one canonical leaf or flower size; reference photography across flower and fruit stages remains desirable.

Sources: USGS Nonindigenous Aquatic Species species profile (morphology, measurements, rhizome, petals and flower behavior), https://nas.er.usgs.gov/Queries/FactSheet.aspx?SpeciesID=251 ; Burke Herbarium / Flora of North America treatment (morphological ranges and reproductive characters), https://burkeherbarium.org/imagecollection/taxon.php?Taxon=Nymphaea+odorata ; University of Florida IFAS Center for Aquatic and Invasive Plants (leaf surface, petiole and habitat), https://plant-directory.ifas.ufl.edu/plant-directory/nymphaea-odorata/ .

## *Pistia stratiotes* — water lettuce

- **Taxonomy:** Araceae; Alismatales; accepted name *Pistia stratiotes* L.
- **Habit and attachment:** free-floating, clonal perennial; no soil-rooted trunk. Short stolons arise from rosette leaf axils and form daughter rosettes, sometimes producing dense floating mats.
- **Leaf construction:** overlapping obovate to spatulate blades in an open rosette; pale green to grey-green, spongy near the base, with conspicuous parallel ribs (commonly 7–15) and dense soft hairs on both surfaces. Published dimensions vary with clone and conditions: commonly about 10–20 cm long; rosettes often 6–30 cm across.
- **Root construction:** dense, feathery fibrous roots hang freely beneath the rosette; the USGS profile reports lengths around 50 cm, with other field references recording longer roots in suitable conditions.
- **Reproductive construction:** tiny axillary spathe-and-spadix inflorescence concealed among central leaf bases; a single female flower lies below several male flowers. Fruit is an inconspicuous green many-seeded berry.
- **Renderer constraints:** show a floating rosette with a shortened/inflated base, ribbed velvety leaves, a root curtain and (where colony form is intended) stolons linking daughter rosettes. Never depict a terrestrial taproot or a conspicuous flower spike.
- **Remaining gap:** size ranges vary substantially with nutrient status, temperature and density; retain ranges and avoid using a single fixed scale. Verify flowering/fruiting season for the intended geographic population.

Sources: USGS Nonindigenous Aquatic Species species profile (leaf, stolon, root and flower dimensions), https://nas.er.usgs.gov/queries/factsheet.aspx?SpeciesID=1099 ; University of Florida IFAS Center for Aquatic and Invasive Plants (species description and reproductive details), https://plant-directory.ifas.ufl.edu/plant-directory/pistia-stratiotes/ ; EPPO Global Database (clonal habit and inflorescence), https://gd.eppo.int/taxon/PIIST/datasheet .

## *Lemna minor* — common duckweed

- **Taxonomy:** Araceae; Alismatales; accepted name *Lemna minor* L.
- **Habit and attachment:** minute free-floating aquatic perennial; no conventional leafy shoot, stem, or rhizome. Fronds remain at the surface and reproduce predominantly by vegetative budding, often persisting as coherent groups.
- **Frond construction:** flat, entire, ovate fronds generally 1–8 mm long and about 1.3–2 times as long as wide; commonly 1–5 or more fronds remain connected. The Flora of North America account records 3 (occasionally 5) veins. Fronds are scarcely gibbous rather than strongly inflated.
- **Root construction:** a simple root hangs from the lower surface of each frond; roots can reach 15 cm. The root sheath is not winged and the root tip is usually rounded.
- **Reproductive construction:** flowering is minute and uncommon; each flower is reduced, and fruits and seeds are also minute. Do not use a visible ornamental flower as a diagnostic feature.
- **Renderer constraints:** render at magnified scale or as a surface colony with a scale cue; retain the tiny individual fronds and simple hanging roots. Do not give it a rosette, stem, or broad foliage.
- **Remaining gap:** confirm local seasonal frond-size variation and colony density; flowering is rare and should be a separate optional reproductive state, not the default plate.

Sources: Burke Herbarium / Flora of North America treatment (frond and root measurements, venation, fruit), https://burkeherbarium.org/imagecollection/taxon.php?Taxon=Lemna+minor ; USDA PLANTS Database (accepted name and distribution), https://plants.sc.egov.usda.gov/home.html .

## Method note

The three records above have species-specific morphology and quantitative characters. Dimensions are intentionally ranges and are not interchangeable among species. The research does not yet establish population-specific seasonal distributions or a single canonical photographic reference set; those remain explicit gaps rather than guessed values.
