# Wood

## Parts

- **Growth rings:** light earlywood and darker latewood, one pair per year.
- **Medullary rays** run from the centre outward.
- **Grain** is the direction of the fibres. **Figure** is the pattern you see on the cut face.

## Distinguishing features (by how the board was sawn)

- **Flat-sawn (plain-sawn):** the most common cut. Its face shows arched or flame-shaped "cathedral" grain, and knots show as small circles.
- **Quarter-sawn:** growth rings at right angles to the face, so the face shows straight lines. Oak shows ray fleck ("tiger stripes"), and knots are long, thin spike knots.
- **Rift-sawn:** straight lines with no ray fleck.
- **Species:**
  - oak: brown, straight-grained, coarse; darkens with age;
  - pine: pale yellow with strong rings and knots;
  - elm and chestnut: brown, with wilder grain.

## Rendering notes

- One board at a time, each with its own seed and a slight shift in colour.
- Cathedral grain: nested arches along the board, made by warping offset lines with noise.
- Knots: a dark ellipse with rings around it; nearby grain bends around it.
- Edges and corners are lighter where worn and darker in the joints; grime collects low down.
